Built next, with you

The modules an operation asks for

Each of these starts as a trial with a launch operator, on your own files and your own rules, and arrives in the same app and the same design as the products above. The list is open: if your operation needs something else, that is the conversation we want.

🛡️

Crewops Safety Safety reporting

A safety, hazard, cabin or fatigue report opened from the crew app, with flight number, aircraft, station, crew and duty times filled in from the roster, photos attached, written offline and sent later. For the safety office: open, overdue and recurring reports, investigation notes, corrective actions, the 72-hour clock and an ECCAIRS-compatible export. A fatigue report lands in Crewops Insight next to the model's prediction for that duty.

🎓

Crewops Academy Training & qualification records

Who took which course, when, with which instructor, and when it expires: recurrent, LPC and OPC, SEP, dangerous goods, CRM, first aid, line checks. The crew member sees it on the phone as My Training, with what is due, read-and-sign bulletins and short courses, checked against the roster. The training department sees the whole fleet's calendar and gaps, keeps the records the regulation asks for, and writes completions back to its training system.

🌙

Fatigue reports & the FRM loop

The crew member files a fatigue report from the phone; it lands in Crewops Insight next to the model's prediction for that duty. Roster pattern analysis, planned versus flown, the fatigue report register and the documentation ORO.FTL asks for, in one place.

📤

Roster publishing

For operators who plan in a spreadsheet: the planner drops the roster, the limit check runs before it goes out, and the roster lands on every crew phone with change notices, the same way the large crew systems deliver it.

📣

Crew communication

Company notices, safety bulletins and revisions delivered to the crew app, with read receipts and acknowledgement tracking, so the operation knows who has seen what before the next duty.

📚

Digital manuals & SOP library

OM-A, OM-B, SOPs, cabin manual, QRH and bulletins as one controlled library on the phone: revision control, read acknowledgement, offline. On top of it an assistant that answers only from your approved documents and cites the section it used.

☢️

Radiation dose records

The cosmic radiation dose per crew member from the flown sectors, the annual record per person, and the threshold notices the operator has to keep. The crew app already computes the dose per flight; this is the operator's side of it.

🔁

Duty requests & swaps

Swap, leave and request bidding from the crew phone, with the limit check run before the request is made and an approval flow on the company side; written back to the crew system where its integration allows it.

🧭

Operations views

What a change does before it is published: the limits, the rest and the predicted alertness of the crew it touches. Disruption notices to the crews concerned, and crew recovery views built on the same engines.

📊

Records & reporting

Flight and duty time records per crew member from the flown roster, cumulative limits over 7, 14 and 28 days and the year, planned-versus-flown rates, and the exports an authority audit or an FRM review asks for. The crew app's logbook engine, run for the whole operation.

🫀

Wellbeing content for crews

The crew app carries a library of sleep, body-clock and fatigue advice cards, each with its evidence grade and source, in seven languages. An operator can add its own guidance to it. What a crew member records about sleep or health stays on the phone; the operation sees the library, never the person.

🔌

Integrations & API

NetLine/CrewLink and JetXBit today, AIMS, Leon and calendar feeds by file or page, and an API for your own systems so the platform reads from and writes to what you already run. A reader for a new system is written from one sample export.

Questions

What operators ask first

Can we start with one module?

Yes. Most operators start with Crewops Insight on their own roster export, or with Crewops Crew for a group of crews. Every further module is added on the same licence, in the same app and the same design, and it starts as a trial on your own files.

Where does the data live?

Crew data stays on the crew member's phone: roster, sleep, fatigue and health data never reach a Hawk & Mason server. Insight runs in the browser on your own computer. Company modules that need a shared record, such as safety reports or training records, run in a workspace set up for your operator, and the data-flow map is part of the proposal.

Do our crews need an account?

No. Crews install Crewops Crew from a link and enter the code your airline holds. No subscription, no account; the code says which airline, until when, and which modules are on.

Which crew systems does it read?

NetLine/CrewLink and JetXBit with live sync; AIMS eCrew, CrewConnex, FLiCA, Leon, CAE Crew Access, RAIDO and Sabre CWP through their roster pages, PDFs, spreadsheets or calendar links; any .ics calendar or roster PDF. A reader for another system is written from one sample export.

Is Crewops Insight an FRMS?

It implements a published biomathematical model and documents its limits. It supports a fatigue risk assessment and the FRM documentation ORO.FTL asks for; it is not an FRMS by itself and it makes no judgement about any person's fitness to fly.

Can you build a module we do not see here?

Tell us what your operation needs. The readers, the engines, the fatigue model, the licence codes and the crew app are shared by every module, so a new one starts from a working base. Write to hello@hawkmason.com with the problem; we answer with a first-version plan.

Crewops is independent. Crew system names belong to their owners and are used only to say which formats the platform reads; no endorsement is implied. Insight implements a published model and is not a fitness-to-fly judgement. Operator systems and records prevail.
