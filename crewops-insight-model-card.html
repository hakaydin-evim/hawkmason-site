Crewops Insight — Model card

Version 0.6.0 · 12 October 2026 · Hawk & Mason Studios · hello@hawkmason.com

This card states what Crewops Insight computes, from which published model, under which assumptions, and what it cannot tell you. The report edition of this card, delivered with every assessment, adds the equations and parameter values so that a compliance team or an authority can follow each figure back to its source.

1. Intended use

Insight reads an operator's roster (planned or flown) and, for each flight duty, predicts the crew member's alertness through the duty and at each top of descent, states the flight duty period against the applicable limit, and summarises the pattern over a month. It supports a fatigue risk assessment, the FRM documentation that ORO.FTL asks for, and the scientific annex of a request to deviate from a certification specification.

It is not a fatigue risk management system by itself, it is not a go/no-go device, and it makes no statement about any individual's fitness to fly. Its sleep is predicted from the roster, not measured. The operator's systems and records prevail.

2. The model

Insight implements the Three-Process Model of alertness (Åkerstedt and Folkard; Åkerstedt, Folkard and Portin, Aviation, Space, and Environmental Medicine, 2004) with the parameters re-estimated on aircrew by Ingre, Van Leeuwen, Klemets, Ullvetter, Hough, Kecklund, Karlsson and Åkerstedt, "Validating and extending the Three Process Model of alertness in airline operations", PLoS ONE 9(10): e108679, 2014. This is the model family behind the Boeing Alertness Model.

Alertness is the sum of the model's three processes (homeostatic sleep pressure, the circadian rhythm and the ultradian afternoon dip), evaluated on a 5-minute grid and converted to the Karolinska Sleepiness Scale (KSS, 1–9) with the published mapping; Samn-Perelli (1–7) is printed beside it with EASA's pairing of KSS 7 with SP 5. The parameter set is the aircrew fit of Ingre et al. 2014, used as published; the equations and values are in the papers cited below and are listed in full in the report edition of this card.

Sleep generator. The roster gives sleep opportunities, not sleep. Outside duty the model puts the person to sleep and wakes them by the published thresholds, never within one hour before a duty, never inside the duty and its margins, and not within two hours of the last waking. Before the analysed window the model settles for a week of free days.

Time zones. The body clock starts on home-base time and absorbs a daily fraction of the remaining offset to the current zone (the aircrew fit; adjustable as a study assumption).

In-flight rest (augmented crews). When the roster lists three or more pilots on a duty, each leg long enough yields a rest share per pilot, computed from the leg's block time by the CS FTL.1.205(c) minimums. The modelled pilot's slot ends two hours before on-blocks (the landing pilot's rest) unless the study assumption says the relief slot. The slot is taken as sleep; by default the whole slot is credited (the favourable bound), and the study assumption can credit a smaller fraction.

Top of descent. 30 minutes before on-blocks by default (20 or 45 selectable). KSS at that instant is the per-landing figure; the per-duty figure is the highest landing.

3. The limits it is checked against

  • FDP: the operator's own ceiling in the file when it prints one; otherwise the ORO.FTL / CS FTL.1.205 table for the acclimatised crew, the CS FTL.1.205(c) augmented-crew values (14/15/16 h with one additional pilot and 15/16/17 h with two, by rest facility class 3/2/1), the extra hour of (c)(4) when one sector exceeds 9 h and the FDP has at most two sectors, and the three-sector limit.
  • Night-duty subtypes of ED Decision 2023/023/R: start 02:00–04:59, end 02:00–05:59, end after 06:00 with a start before 02:00.
  • Risk markers of EASA's FTL 2.0 research: KSS ≥ 7 and Samn-Perelli ≥ 5 at top of descent.
  • Prior sleep/wake score (PSWM) of GM5 CS FTL.1.205.
  • Planned versus flown: the share of duties exceeding the planned FDP, for ORO.FTL.110(j).

4. Verification and validation status

  • Implementation check. A test suite runs the model against the published anchor values (the KSS mapping, the sleep thresholds, the daily adaptation, the shape of a day's curve); the FDP tables are tested against the published limits.
  • Comparison with another model. Planned with FTL Airlines (October 2026): the same rotation run under the same stated assumptions, every difference attributed to its cause. Not yet done.
  • Comparison with crew ratings. The crew app records a Samn-Perelli rating after each flight; the model's prediction can be set beside it once an operator's pilots use the app. Not yet done.
  • What has not been done. No independent review of this implementation; no published validation of this implementation against measured sleep or performance. The model it implements has that literature (Ingre 2014: 136 aircrews, and EASA's FTL 2.0 study compared models of this family on 7,102 observations); the implementation does not yet.

5. Known limitations

  • Sleep is predicted from opportunities; actual sleep in hotels, bunks and at home is not measured and varies between people. This is the first limitation to state in any file.
  • One set of parameters for everyone: no chronotype, age or individual sensitivity. The aircrew fit is for the intermediate chronotype.
  • No workload, no light exposure, no caffeine or naps outside the generator's rules, no commuting.
  • Bunk rest is credited as sleep of the chosen fraction; the quality of in-flight sleep is not modelled beyond that fraction.
  • The circadian adaptation is a daily step towards the local zone; it does not model partial or directional (east/west) asymmetries.
  • The flown-versus-planned comparison needs the flown file in the same format; it does not read actual sleep.
  • Readers for roster formats are built from samples; a format read for the first time is flagged and should be checked against the roster by eye.

6. How to cite a run

State: Crewops Insight version; the model (Three-Process Model, Ingre 2014 parameters); the assumptions the run printed (in-flight rest credited or not, facility class, rest slot, fraction credited, sleep before the rotation, adaptation rate, top-of-descent lead); the rule set; the file(s) analysed and their dates. The CSV export carries the assumptions in every row.
