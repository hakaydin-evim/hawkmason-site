Crewops Insight — Model card

Version 0.6.1 · 15 October 2026 · Hawk & Mason Studios · hello@hawkmason.com

This card states what Crewops Insight computes, from which published model, under which assumptions, and what it cannot tell you. The report edition of this card, delivered with an assessment report, adds the equations and parameter values so that a reader can follow each figure back to its source.

1. Intended use

Insight reads an operator's roster (planned or flown) and, for each flight duty, predicts the crew member's alertness through the duty and at a stand-in for top of descent (30 minutes before each on-blocks by default), sets the flight duty period against the ceiling in use (the one the file prints, otherwise the table of the selected rule set), and summarises the pattern over a month. It is an evaluation tool that supports an operator's own fatigue risk assessment; it does not replace the operator's FRM process or its documentation.

It is not a fatigue risk management system by itself, it is not a go/no-go device, and it makes no statement about any individual's fitness to fly. Its sleep is predicted from the roster, not measured. The within and over labels compare an FDP with the ceiling in use (the label reads n/a where nothing can be assessed); they are not a compliance statement. The operator's systems and records prevail.

Where it runs. Roster files are read and analysed in the browser tab and are not uploaded. The PDF and spreadsheet readers load from a public CDN, and the fonts from Google Fonts, when the page opens. With a licence code the page also sends usage counts (opens, panel views, analyses and exports) to our server and, when crew ratings are included, asks our ratings service for the ratings between the first and last date of the roster analysed; the licence code goes with each request and stays in the browser until sign-out. Two things are kept on our server: counts of portal use (opens, panel views, analyses and exports, each with date, time, the airline and whether the operator or crew code was used, kept for 400 days) and crew ratings (section 5, kept for two years).

2. The model

Insight implements the Three-Process Model of alertness (Åkerstedt and Folkard; Åkerstedt, Folkard and Portin, Aviation, Space, and Environmental Medicine, 2004). Two parameters come from the aircrew study of Ingre, Van Leeuwen, Klemets, Ullvetter, Hough, Kecklund, Karlsson and Åkerstedt, "Validating and extending the Three Process Model of alertness in airline operations", PLoS ONE 9(10): e108679, 2014 (136 aircrews): the circadian phase of an intermediate chronotype, and a time-zone adaptation of 30 per cent of the remaining offset per day. The other constants are the original model's, including the ultradian component, the sleep thresholds (8.38 and 11.38) and the conversion to KSS; Ingre et al. fitted other values for some of these (for example KSS constants of 9.68 and −0.46 with the default circadian phase, and sleep thresholds of about 8 and 13 with circadian-type phases), which Insight does not use. Ingre et al. report that the original sleep generator did not perform well with adjusted circadian phases, which is the pairing Insight uses: an adjusted phase with the original thresholds. The model family is the one behind the Boeing Alertness Model.

Alertness is the sum of the model's three processes (homeostatic sleep pressure, the circadian rhythm and the ultradian afternoon dip), evaluated on a 5-minute grid and converted to the Karolinska Sleepiness Scale (KSS, 1–9) with the original model's mapping, KSS = 10.6 − 0.6 × alertness. Samn-Perelli (1–7) is not modelled separately. Insight prints an equivalent, the KSS rounded and reduced by 2, so that KSS 7 pairs with SP 5 and KSS 8 with SP 6, the pairs EASA uses. EASA's own regression of Samn-Perelli on KSS (FTL 2.0 deliverable D2.5: SP ≈ 0.98 + 0.59 × KSS) gives somewhat different values, for example about 3.9 for KSS 5 where Insight gives 3, so a reported-minus-predicted difference carries that offset. The equations and values are in the papers cited below and are listed in full in the report edition of this card.

Sleep generator. The roster gives sleep opportunities, not sleep. Outside duty the model puts the person to sleep and wakes them by the original model's thresholds, never within one hour before a duty, never inside the duty and its margins, and not within two hours of the last waking. Before the analysed window the model settles for a week of free days.

Time zones. The body clock starts on home-base time and absorbs a daily fraction of the remaining offset to the current zone (30 per cent per day by default; adjustable as a study assumption).

In-flight rest (augmented crews). When the roster lists three or more pilots on a duty, each leg long enough yields a rest share per pilot, computed from the leg's block time (never under the 90-minute minimum of CS FTL.1.205(c)). By default the modelled pilot's slot is the landing pilot's and ends two hours before on-blocks; the study assumption can choose the relief slot, which starts one hour after departure. The slot is taken as sleep; by default the whole slot is credited (the favourable bound), and the study assumption can credit a smaller fraction. The rest facility class you select does not change this credit; it selects the CS FTL.1.205(c) maximum FDP shown beside the duty (the default is class 1, the most permissive, and the class is not read from the file). Where the file does not list the pilots, no rest is credited. Running a roster with in-flight rest credited and not credited gives the range.

Top of descent. A roster does not give the aircraft's top of descent, so Insight takes a fixed lead before on-blocks: 30 minutes by default (20 or 45 selectable). KSS at that instant is the per-landing figure; the per-duty figure is the highest landing, and the page and the CSV show only that value per duty. EASA's metric is taken at the last top of descent of the duty.

3. The limits and markers it is read against

  • FDP: the ceiling the file prints, when it prints one (the operator's scheme prevails); otherwise the table of the selected rule set: for EASA the CS FTL.1.205 table for a two-pilot crew; the SHGM and UK CAA selections use the same table and national differences are not modelled; FAA uses Part 117 Table B, unaugmented. These tables were transcribed from secondary sources compiled during research, not read line by line from the regulation text, and no independent compliance reviewer has checked them; the Crewops app marks them unverified. The within and over label compares the FDP with that ceiling. Where a duty lists three or four pilots and the file prints no ceiling, the label compares the FDP with the two-pilot table, so a duty inside its CS FTL.1.205(c) maximum can read over; it then also counts in the "FDP over the maximum in use" tile and in the planned-versus-flown share. Standby and ground duties also appear in the table; their FDP, maximum and label come from the flight-duty table, are not FDP assessments and are not counted in the tiles.
  • Augmented crews: for duties that list three or four pilots (pilots are the crew entries not marked as cabin crew), the CS FTL.1.205(c) maximum for the rest facility class selected (14/15/16 h with one additional pilot and 15/16/17 h with two, by class 3/2/1; the class is not read from the file), plus one hour when one sector is over 9 h block time and the FDP has at most two sectors (deadhead legs count as sectors), is shown beside the ceiling for comparison. It does not replace the ceiling behind the within or over label, and no such value is shown for other crew sizes.
  • Night-duty subtypes of CS FTL.1.205(a)(3) as amended by ED Decision 2023/023/R: start 02:00–04:59; end 02:00–05:59 with a start at 01:59 or earlier; end at 06:00 or later with a start at 01:59 or earlier. Each flight duty is placed by its report and release time on the home-base clock. Insight reads "start at 01:59 or earlier" as including starts from 18:00, and requires a duty of at least six hours for the last subtype; both are Insight's choices, not the text of the decision. Duties other than flight duties are not classified.
  • High-fatigue ratings that EASA's FTL 2.0 research recommends as safety metrics, measured on crew ratings at the last top of descent of non-augmented duties: KSS 7 or higher and Samn-Perelli 5 or higher. Insight applies them to its predicted KSS at the top-of-descent stand-in and to the Samn-Perelli equivalent of section 2, so the two can differ near the threshold (KSS 6.5 to 6.9 shows SP 5, while the count of duties above threshold uses KSS 7.0 and over). They are research metrics, not regulatory limits. The night subtypes, the CS FTL.1.205(c) maximum and these markers are EASA-based under every rule set.
  • Prior sleep/wake score: Insight's own score, shown as points (0 to 12), from predicted sleep in the 24 and 48 hours before report (which includes credited in-flight rest) and the hours awake at report. It is not the scoring of GM5 CS FTL.1.205, whose point table differs; no risk band is applied to it.
  • Planned versus flown: the share of matched flight duties whose flown FDP exceeds the maximum in use (the printed ceiling or the table value, with the caveat above for augmented duties), shown beside the 33 per cent level of ORO.FTL.110(j); the page flags it above one third. Duties are matched by date, type, start station and first flight number, and only FDP is compared. The rule applies to a schedule over a scheduled seasonal period, so the share is only as complete as the files supplied.

4. Verification and validation status

  • Implementation check. A test suite checks the conversion from alertness to KSS, the pairing of KSS 7 with Samn-Perelli 5 and KSS 8 with 6, the plausibility of the generated sleep and of the shape of a day's curve, and the augmented-crew limits as transcribed. It does not compare predictions with published predicted values. The FDP tables are transcribed from secondary sources and have not been reviewed line by line.
  • Comparison with another model. Planned with an operator (October 2026): the same rotation run under the same stated assumptions as the operator's existing analysis, every difference attributed to its cause. Not yet done.
  • Comparison with crew ratings. The crew app records a Samn-Perelli rating after each flight, and crew members of airlines on the ratings programme can share them; the page sets them beside the Samn-Perelli equivalent. That comparison has not been carried out as a validation. Not yet done.
  • What has not been done. No independent review of this implementation; no published validation of this implementation against measured sleep or performance. The model family has literature: Ingre et al. 2014 evaluated the model on 136 aircrews and report a residual error of about 1.4 KSS points (standard deviation) for their best-fitting models on the crews' logged sleep, and about 1.5 for one variant with model-generated sleep. They did not test the parameter mix used here (an adjusted circadian phase with the original sleep thresholds). EASA's FTL 2.0 study (deliverable D2.4) compared the Boeing Alertness Model with SAFTE (7,102 observations) and with reported KSS; it did not examine this implementation, and it states that it does not aim to validate sleep predictions. Insight's own prediction error has not been measured.

5. Known limitations

  • Sleep is predicted from opportunities; actual sleep in hotels, bunks and at home is not measured and varies between people. This is the first limitation to state in any file.
  • One set of parameters for everyone: no chronotype, age or individual sensitivity. The circadian phase is the one for the intermediate chronotype.
  • No workload, no light exposure, no caffeine or naps outside the generator's rules, no commuting.
  • In-flight rest is credited as sleep, by default the whole slot (the favourable bound) or the fraction chosen; the rest facility and the quality of in-flight sleep are not modelled beyond that fraction. Rest is credited only where the roster lists three or more pilots.
  • The circadian adaptation is a daily step towards the local zone; it does not model partial or directional (east/west) asymmetries.
  • The flown-versus-planned comparison needs the flown file in the same format. It compares FDP only, matches duties by date, type, start station and first flight number (a duty whose first flight number changed drops out of the share), shows alertness for the planned roster only, and does not read actual sleep.
  • Only two readers were built from real exports: NetLine/Crew duty plans and the "Activités" rotation workbook (.xlsx). The others (Leon, CAE Crew Access, Lufthansa CAS and FLiCA PDFs; AIMS eCrew page and CSV; CrewConnex and Sabre CWP pages; SkedPlus+; the RAIDO/NAVBLUE and FLiCA calendar layouts) were built from public format descriptions or test files and are beta. The page does not mark duties read by a beta reader, so a first read should be checked against the roster by eye. The browser reads PDF text with pdf.js. The NetLine reader has been checked on two real PDFs; other layouts have not. A calendar (.ics) file that carries no report time is read from the first departure, so its FDP is counted from departure, not from report.
  • Crew ratings shown beside the prediction are self-reported by crew members of airlines on the ratings programme, who must switch sharing on (it is off by default). Each rating carries the flight, date, route, number of sectors, the rating itself and when it was given, the role when known and, when entered, the crew code, plus a random identifier of the pilot's phone so that a changed rating replaces the earlier one; ratings are not anonymous to the holder of the airline's operator code. The service that receives them does not verify who sent a rating. They are kept on our server for two years and read back with the operator code or an API key made from it.

6. How to cite a run

State: Crewops Insight version; the model (Three-Process Model, Ingre 2014 circadian phase and adaptation, other constants as in the original); the assumptions the run printed (in-flight rest credited or not, facility class, rest slot, fraction credited, sleep before the rotation, adaptation rate, top-of-descent lead); the rule set and home base; the file(s) analysed and their dates. The CSV export carries the assumptions in every row; it does not name the rule set, home base or Insight version, so add them when you cite a run.
