Summary

Planned versus flown

Duties are matched by date, type, start station and first flight number; a duty whose first flight number changed is not matched. Only FDP is compared; alertness is shown for the planned roster. "Max" is the ceiling the file prints, or the table value when it prints none; for a duty with three or four listed pilots and no printed ceiling that is the two-pilot table value, so a duty can count as flown over the maximum while inside its augmented limit. ORO.FTL.110(j) sets a 33% level for a schedule over a scheduled seasonal period, so the share shown is only as complete as the files supplied; the tile reads YES above one third.

Duties

KSS 1–9 (Karolinska Sleepiness Scale) is predicted 30 minutes before each on-blocks (adjustable), taken as the stand-in for top of descent; a duty row shows the highest of its landings. "SP equiv. TOD" is a Samn-Perelli equivalent: the KSS rounded, minus 2. Samn-Perelli is not modelled separately, so it can differ from a rating given on that scale. KSS ≥ 7 and Samn-Perelli ≥ 5 are high-fatigue ratings that EASA's FTL 2.0 research recommends as safety metrics, measured on crew ratings at the last top of descent of non-augmented duties (Insight applies them to every flight duty); they are research metrics, not regulatory limits, and the two columns can disagree near the threshold (KSS 6.5 to 6.9 shows SP 5, while the KSS count above counts 7.0 and over). Sleep is predicted from the roster, not measured. The source study reported a residual error of about 1.4 KSS points (standard deviation) for its best-fitting models on the crews' logged sleep, and about 1.5 for one variant with model-generated sleep. It did not test the parameter mix used here, and Insight's own prediction error has not been measured.

"Max FDP" is the ceiling the file prints; where the file prints none, it is the table of the selected rule set: for EASA, the CS FTL.1.205 table for a two-pilot crew; the SHGM and UK CAA selections use the same table (national differences are not modelled); FAA uses Part 117 Table B, unaugmented. These tables were transcribed from secondary sources, not read line by line from the regulation text, and no independent compliance reviewer has checked them. "Within / over" compares the FDP with that ceiling (where nothing can be assessed the label reads n/a); it is not a compliance statement, and the operator's own scheme prevails. Where a duty lists three or four pilots and the file prints no ceiling, that label compares the FDP with the two-pilot table, so a duty inside its CS FTL.1.205(c) maximum can read over; it then also counts in "FDP over the maximum in use" and in the planned-versus-flown share. The table also lists standby and ground duties; their FDP, Max FDP and within label come from the flight-duty table, are not FDP assessments and are not counted in the tiles.

For duties that list three or four pilots ("Pilots" counts the crew entries not marked as cabin crew), "EASA max (aug.)" shows beside it the CS FTL.1.205(c) maximum for the selected rest facility class (14/15/16 h with one additional pilot and 15/16/17 h with two, class 3/2/1; the class is your choice and is not read from the file), plus 1 h when one sector is over 9 h block time and the FDP has at most 2 sectors, (c)(4), deadhead legs included. It is for comparison and does not change the within / over label; read it beside that label. No value is shown for other crew sizes. Where the file lists three or more pilots, "Rest" is the in-flight rest credited to this pilot as sleep in the alertness run: by default all of the slot (the favourable bound), or the fraction chosen under study assumptions. By default the slot is the landing pilot's and ends two hours before on-blocks; the relief slot starts one hour after departure. The facility class does not change it. Run the duty with rest credited and not credited to see the range.

"Night" places each flight duty, by report and release time on the home-base clock, in the three night-duty subtypes of CS FTL.1.205(a)(3) as amended by ED Decision 2023/023/R: start 02:00–04:59 · end 02:00–05:59 · end 06:00 or later (the last two with a start at 01:59 or earlier). Insight reads "start at 01:59 or earlier" as including starts from 18:00, and requires a duty of at least 6 hours for the last subtype. "Sleep/wake score" is Insight's own score, shown as points (0 to 12), from predicted sleep in the 24 and 48 hours before report (which includes credited in-flight rest) and the hours awake at report; it is not the scoring of GM5 CS FTL.1.205, whose point table is different, and no risk band is applied to it. "Night", "EASA max (aug.)" and the KSS 7 / Samn-Perelli 5 markers are EASA-based under every rule set.

Crew-reported, by destination

Samn-Perelli ratings that crew members of your airline chose to share from the Crewops app after the flight. Sharing is off by default and is offered only to airlines on the ratings programme. Each rating carries the flight, date, route, number of sectors, the rating itself and when it was given, the role when known and, when the pilot entered one, the crew code, plus a random identifier of the pilot's phone, so that a changed rating replaces the earlier one. Ratings are not anonymous to the holder of your operator code: the crew code, when entered, names the pilot. Ratings are self-reported, the receiving service does not verify who sent one, and they are kept on our server for two years; they are read back with your airline's operator code or an API key made from it. A rating given on the KSS scale is converted to Samn-Perelli as KSS minus 2. The predicted figure is Insight's Samn-Perelli equivalent (KSS minus 2) at the highest-KSS landing of the same duty, matched by date and flight number. The difference is reported minus predicted: positive means the rating is higher (sleepier) than the prediction. The comparison is indicative; the prediction has not been validated against these ratings.

By month

What this is. A roster reading with two layers: the duty-factor indicator Crewops shows crew (0–100) and a biomathematical prediction of alertness through each duty. The method is documented for licensed operators and provided on request.

What it is not. Not a measurement of any person, not a validated commercial product, not a fitness-to-fly judgement and not an FRMS by itself. Per CASA (2010, 2014) and the IATA Human Factors Task Force (2025): model outputs support a risk assessment and compare rosters relatively; any threshold must be validated in your own operation; never a sole go/no-go. No independent review of this implementation and no validation against measured sleep has been done yet. The FTL tables were transcribed from secondary sources, not read line by line from the regulation text, and are marked unverified in the Crewops app; your operator's scheme and system prevail.

Where the data goes. The roster files are read and analysed in this browser tab and are not uploaded; close the tab and the analysis is gone. The PDF and spreadsheet readers load from a public CDN (cdnjs) and the fonts from Google Fonts when the page opens. With a licence code, the page also sends usage counts (opens, panel views, analyses and exports) to our server and, when "Include crew-reported ratings" is ticked, asks our ratings service for the ratings between the first and last date of the roster you analysed; the licence code goes with each request and stays in this browser until you sign out. Two things are kept on our server: counts of portal use (opens, panel views, analyses and exports, each with date, time, your airline and whether the operator or crew code was used, kept for 400 days) and crew ratings (the crew app sends them, with the crew member's consent, and they are kept for two years).
