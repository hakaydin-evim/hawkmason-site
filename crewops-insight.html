Planned versus flown

Duties

KSS 1–9 (Karolinska Sleepiness Scale) and Samn-Perelli 1–7 predicted at top of descent; EASA FTL 2.0 risk markers are KSS ≥ 7 and SP ≥ 5. Sleep is predicted from the roster, not measured. For augmented crews (three or more pilots listed) "EASA max" is the CS FTL.1.205(c) value for that crew and rest facility: 14/15/16 h with one additional pilot and 15/16/17 h with two (class 3/2/1), plus 1 h when one sector exceeds 9 h and the FDP has at most 2 sectors (c)(4); 3 sectors at most; 90 min rest each, 2 h for the landing pilots and "Rest" is the in-flight rest credited to this pilot as sleep, ending two hours before on-blocks; the operator's own ceiling prevails where an approved scheme sets it. "Night" follows ED Decision 2023/023/R: start 02:00–04:59 · end 02:00–05:59 · end after 06:00 (start before 02:00). PSWM is the GM5 CS FTL.1.205 prior-sleep/wake score (0 none · 1–4 low · 5–8 moderate · 9+ high).

Crew-reported, by destination

Samn-Perelli as the crew gave it after the flight, from the Crewops app, with the crew member's own switch on; the predicted figure is the model's SP at the worst landing of the same duty. The difference is reported minus predicted: positive means the crew felt sleepier than the model says.

Model card

The full model card (model, equations, parameters, assumptions, verification status, limitations, references) is at hawkmason.com/crewops/insight/model-card; it accompanies every assessment report.

What this is. A roster reading with two layers: the duty-factor indicator Crewops shows crew (WOCL, early start, long FDP, sectors, short rest, consecutive duties, 7-day load, time zones; 0–100) and a biomathematical prediction from the Three-Process Model of alertness (Åkerstedt & Folkard; parameters re-estimated on 136 aircrews by Ingre et al. 2014, PLoS ONE 9(10):e108679), the same model family behind the Boeing Alertness Model. Sleep is generated from the duty pattern with the paper's thresholds; time-zone adaptation 30 % per day; KSS = 10.6 − 0.6 × alertness.

What it is not. Not a measurement of any person, not a validated commercial product, not a fitness-to-fly judgement and not an FRMS by itself. Per CASA (2010, 2014) and the IATA Human Factors Task Force (2025): model outputs support a risk assessment and compare rosters relatively; any threshold must be validated in your own operation; never a sole go/no-go. FTL tables are implemented from the published regulation and marked unverified in the Crewops app; your operator's scheme and system prevail.

Where the data goes. Nowhere. Parsing and modelling run in this page; close the tab and it is gone.
