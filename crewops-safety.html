The problem

Why reports go unwritten

Today

A report is a web form behind a company login, or a paper form in the crew room, filled in days later from memory.

The details

Flight number, date, route and duty times are typed again by hand, and the safety office corrects them later.

The reporter

Rarely hears what happened to the report, so the next one is not written.

With Crewops Safety

The report starts from the duty on the phone, can be written offline, and the reporter follows its status and reads the safety office's reply on the same screen.

On the crew phone

What your crews see

Inside the crew app your crews already use for their roster, in seven languages.

Sixteen kinds of report

Twelve safety reports: flight operations, cabin, ground and cargo, security, dangerous goods, hazard, technical, health and safety at work, fatigue, bird and wildlife, commander's discretion, suggestion. Four operational reports: irregularity, environment, quality, operations control.

Three steps

Choose the kind of report, pick the duty from the roster, answer that kind's own questions and write what happened. Date, flight, route and duty times come from the roster.

The questions that matter

Each kind asks its own: phase of flight and the event list for a flight report, the disruptive level and what was done on board for an unruly passenger, UN number and class for dangerous goods, injury, body part and treatment at work, birds seen and struck and the parts hit for a bird strike. Tap what you know; the safety office completes the rest.

Commander's discretion

The planned and maximum FDP come from the roster and the FTL engine, the actual from the on-blocks or the commander's entry, and the discretion used is worked out. The commander adds the reason and the report, as ORO.FTL.205(f) asks.

Fatigue report

Starts from the duty with its FDP against the limit and the number of sectors. The crew member gives a Samn-Perelli score and picks the reasons: short rest, night duty, long FDP, early start, poor hotel sleep, delay, time zones.

Without my name

A report can be sent without the crew code or name. It still holds the flight and duty it came from and a random phone identifier, which is how the reply reaches you; both sit in the safety office's record. Treat it as confidential, not untraceable.

Status and reply

Sent, under review, action taken, closed: each step is on the phone with its time. The safety office's reply and its questions arrive there, and the crew member can add information.

Photos

Up to four photos per report, from the camera or the photo library. The safety office sees them with the report.

Offline

Written in a hotel with no signal or on the apron with the phone in flight mode, the report waits on the phone and goes out when it is back online.

Under My airline

Reporting sits on the Me screen under My airline, below the logbook, next to My training and the Library when your airline holds them. No account and no password: crews type the company code in the app.

No app? A web link

Crew members without the app report through your airline's web report link. The page is in English and Turkish and offers every kind of report except commander's discretion, which needs the roster. It takes up to four photos, can be sent without a name, and a follow-up page shows the status and the reply and takes more information.

In the operator portal

What your department does

In its own panel of the operator portal. Your airline's operator code opens it, and a code can open this product alone. Reports, investigations, risks, audits, changes and indicators in one place. Nothing to install.

The report box

Open reports, reports due within 24 hours on the 72-hour clock, fatigue and operational reports, closed and anonymous counts, with safety and operational reports filtered apart. Every report with its kind, date, flight and status, the reporter's own answers labelled under it and the photos. Reports sent through the web link arrive the same way, marked web.

Classification

Status from new through triage, investigation and action to closed. Occurrence class, ADREP occurrence category from the full list, event type at its top level, mandatory reportable under Regulation (EU) 2015/1018, risk class on the ERCS scheme.

Investigation

Root cause, contributing factors, the 5 whys and the findings, kept with the report. The investigation is never shown to the reporter.

Actions and timeline

Corrective and preventive actions with an owner, closed when done. A timeline of everything that happened to the report, for the investigation file and the audit.

Reply and ask

A reply goes straight to the reporter's phone, and so does a request for more information. An internal note stays in the portal and is never shown to the reporter.

Risk register

Each hazard on the ICAO Doc 9859 5×5 matrix with its tolerability, mitigations with an owner and a due date, the residual risk, a review date and the reports it came from.

Audits and findings

Findings at level 1, level 2 or observation, as ORO.GEN.150 sets them out, each with a corrective action plan, an owner, a due date, evidence and closure.

Management of change

What changes, its hazards and the risks it touches, then approval or rejection and a review. Built around ORO.GEN.200(a)(3).

Today board

What needs action now, in one list: reports whose 72-hour clock has passed or ends within 24 hours, urgent reports not yet opened, overdue actions, open intolerable risks, overdue risk reviews and mitigations, overdue findings with level 1 first, and changes not approved close to their date. Each line opens its record.

CSV and ECCAIRS E5X

The report register as CSV in ECCAIRS-style columns. An E5X file per report, or one file of all reports marked mandatory-reportable, checked against the public ECCAIRS data-bridge schema. Your office uploads it to the ECCAIRS 2 reporting portal or your authority's ECCAIRS-based system and completes the operator, aircraft model and aerodrome codes there. The submission is yours; Crewops does not submit.

Your web report link

Alone or connected

Regulatory basis

Built on the rules you work to

The fields, the clock, the classification and the safety office's records are built around the rules your safety office already works to. Your procedures and records prevail.

  • Regulation (EU) 376/2014: occurrence reporting, the 72-hour reporting window and the Annex I data fields
  • Regulation (EU) 2015/1018: the list of occurrences that must be reported
  • Regulation (EU) 2020/2034: the European Risk Classification Scheme (ERCS)
  • ICAO Doc 9859: the 5×5 risk matrix of the risk register
  • ICAO Annex 19 · ORO.GEN.200: hazard identification and internal safety reporting in the management system
  • ORO.GEN.200(a)(3): management of change
  • ORO.GEN.150: audit findings, their levels and corrective action
  • ORO.FTL.120: fatigue reports as an input to fatigue risk management

Questions

What departments ask first

What can crews report?

Sixteen kinds of report: twelve safety reports, from an air safety report to a commander's discretion report, and four operational reports for irregularities, environment, quality and operations control. The 72-hour clock starts on every safety report except a suggestion and stops when the office marks it not reportable. Operational reports and suggestions have none.

Is Crewops Safety a complete SMS?

It puts the records an SMS runs on in one place: reporting, investigation, the risk register, audits and findings, and management of change, with indicators beside them. Your SMS manual, your safety review board and your approvals remain yours; Crewops Safety keeps the records they work from.

Who can read a report?

Your safety office, in the operator portal, with your airline's operator code. Reports are stored on Cloudflare servers that Hawk & Mason Studios runs, with each airline's records kept apart by its airline code and kept until you ask us to delete them. An anonymous report carries no crew code or name, and the reply still comes back to the phone that sent it.

Does it replace our reporting system?

It runs beside your SMS or feeds it. Signed webhooks tell your SMS when a report is created or updated, API keys connect your other systems, and the CSV in ECCAIRS-style columns and the ECCAIRS E5X file carry reports into the occurrence database you already keep.

Can crews report without the app?

Yes. Share your airline's web report link on your crew portal, by email or as a QR code in the crew room. The page is in English and Turkish, offers every kind of report except commander's discretion, which needs the roster and stays in the app, takes up to four photos and can be sent without a name. After sending, a follow-up page shows the status and the reply and takes more information.

What do our crews need?

The crew app and your airline's company code. No account and no password. Crews of an airline that has not bought Crewops Safety do not see Safety in the app.
